APP Development

Improve human communication by humanizing AI-generated text

Most AI humanizers try to change how text looks. HumanizedAI tries to measure why it is tiring to read, rewrite those measured properties, and prove that the rewrite did not alter what the document says.

1Repository

github.com/jingxulabs/HumanizedAI

Python, MIT licensed. The calibration ships prebuilt, so there is nothing to train before a first run. A FastAPI web interface and a command line covering score, signals, features, run, serve and calibrate. The rewriter and the judge run on different vendors' models by design.

2Project overview and summary

Problem. Machine-written prose wears people out. Everything sounds the same, nothing surprises, and attention goes. Communication nobody attends to has lost its purpose. So the target is readability, not disguise: the tool ships no detector, never optimizes against one, and where reading well and looking less machine-made pull apart it follows readability. It also won't make anything true, since a faithful rewrite of a wrong original stays wrong.

Approach. 31 features are calibrated, each scored by how cleanly it separates a labelled human corpus from an AI one. 20 of them separate the two well enough to be picked as steering signals. On a given document a steering signal only becomes an instruction if it clears three filters (separation ≥ 0.65, normalized gap ≥ 0.15, and agreement on direction), and at most six are issued per rewrite, strongest discriminator first. The rewriter then builds a ledger of the assertions in the source and writes fresh prose from that ledger rather than editing sentences: the ledger is fixed, the prose is free. Seven deterministic gates can veto any candidate; an LLM judge running on a different vendor's model advises but cannot.

Result. The human score of AI-generated text improves significantly after a pass through the tool. The shallow-rewrite failure was the finding that mattered. Across 22 saved candidates the earlier pipeline returned text 88–96% word-identical to its input, with verbatim spans up to 276 words, and not one gate ever rejected it. After the ledger change, on the same document and model: word similarity 0.90 → 0.61, depth 0.14 → 0.58, surviving sentences 74% → 19%, longest copied span 89 → 27 words, with literals and meaning still passing. Widening the brief beyond the ten-feature starter set took execution from 27% to 59%.

3The twenty steering signals

Calibration runs over 248 human-written documents and 220 AI-written ones, computing all 31 features across both. The two corpora do different jobs. The human corpus defines the target range: for each feature the calibration stores human percentiles (p10, p25, p50, p75, p90). The AI corpus never defines that range. It establishes only how sharply a feature discriminates between the two populations.

Of those 31 features, 20 separate human from machine writing well enough to steer a rewrite. Separation is an AUC-like measure: 0.50 means no useful discrimination, 1.00 means perfect separation. The system follows the calibrated direction rather than intuition.

Gates

A gate is a pass or fail check that can reject a candidate rewrite outright. Gates veto; the score only ranks whatever survived them. Four properties define one in this codebase.

  1. It vetoes, it does not rank. Gates run before ranking. A candidate that fails one is discarded, no matter how well it scores.
  2. It tests an invariant, not a target. Violating it means the rewrite is wrong, a dropped figure or a third of the length gone, not merely less good. A missed style target ranks a candidate instead of discarding it.
  3. It is deterministic and cheap. No LLM calls, so every candidate gets every gate.
  4. It has a tier. literals and meaning are integrity gates: they protect content a reader cannot check by eye. The rest protect shape. When the pipeline has to ship the least-bad candidate, integrity wins.

5Applications of the tool

Elevate your social media, email, and digital communications with messaging that feels more natural, engaging, and human. The tool helps you align your content with proven human communication patterns, so your message connects more effectively with your audience.

6Competitor analysis and next step

Measured against the humanizer tools already on GitHub, HumanizedAI is one of the more technically ambitious designs in the category, particularly around verification and semantic integrity. I do not see many that match its combination of corpus-derived steering, enforced rewrite depth, deterministic integrity gates and a semantic audit.

The large existing projects still beat it today on adoption and convenience. The gap is not the method. It is everything around the method, and that is the work:

  • Maturity. Several thresholds were tuned while watching a single document fail, the control run over human fixtures is still outstanding, and negation remains invisible to the meaning gate at 0.995.
  • Installation. Too much setup stands between a curious reader and a first run.
  • English only. The feature set and the calibration both assume English.
  • Comparative evaluation. No broad head-to-head against the tools it claims to differ from, which is exactly the claim that most needs evidence.
  • Distribution and interface. The incumbents win on both, and adoption turns on those rather than on method.

Cabinet, your personal decision intelligence

A private council for your life. Consequential decisions fail in ways a chat window makes worse: one confident voice where there should be several, and no record of what you claimed. Cabinet seats 51 advisers across 10 tables, and then scores you against your own confidence. The model is interchangeable. The record is not.

1Build

React and FastAPI, with three model providers behind one interface. A fully functional offline mode means the product demonstrates with no credentials and no network, so nothing here depends on a key being live.

2The problem

Problem. Consequential decisions fail in three ways that conversational AI makes worse, not better. One confident voice where there should be several. No memory of what you claimed, so you never learn whether your 80% sure means anything. And an answer to the question as posed rather than the one you are avoiding.

Position. Cabinet treats this as a structural problem, not a model problem. Every part of the system that matters is the part a better model would not fix.

3The design: a council that disagrees with itself

A standing council, seated across ten tables by domain. 51 seats, and each bench is deliberately composed to disagree with itself. A council that nods is a chat window in costume.

The ten tables: Personal, Finance, Career, Education, AI, Risk, Health, Mind, Family, Leadership.

The round. Pose, fan out, synthesise, push back, decide, review. Five seats answer the same question concurrently and in isolation; none sees another's answer. A synthesis then names the agreement, the real fault line beneath the disagreement, what nobody raised, and what you should resolve before deciding. You can press one adviser privately, put it to the whole table, or re-run the round.

4Demo

Two screens from the running application. The first is the Leadership table being convened; the second is a round in progress, with each seat answering under its own badge.

5The feedback loop, which is the actual product

Every decision is recorded with a verdict, a confidence number, and a date to revisit it. When that date arrives you score the outcome, and the number moves.

Two rules are enforced in the system, not left to good intentions:

  • Confidence is sealed. Once an outcome is recorded, the confidence you claimed cannot be amended.
  • Entries are struck, not edited. An entry can be struck off whole, never quietly revised.

Everything on the intelligence view is computed from the record, not asked of a model. A model can tell you what to do; only your own history can tell you how much to trust yourself when you say seventy per cent.

6AI and human seats, together

Every seat is AI-answered; what differs is whether a real person stands behind it. Simulated figures are badged as simulated wherever they speak. Seats representing people in your life answer immediately as an explicitly labelled stand-in, and are superseded the moment the real person replies, by private link or by you recording what they told you. A third of the council sitting silent is worse than an attributed guess.

This is the bridge: the council is useful on day one with nobody else's participation, and becomes more truthful as they join.

7Position, and what is weak

Four things already do part of this job. The column that separates them is the last one, which is in each case a limit of structure rather than of effort or model quality.

AlternativeStrengthStructural limit
General AI assistant Breadth, zero setup One voice; nothing recorded; no calibration
Persona prompt packs Instant personas Personas in one context converge; no track record
Multi-agent frameworks Genuine parallelism Built for task completion, not for your judgement
Decision journals An honest record No counsel at all; high friction, so abandoned
Cabinet (this work) Counsel and record together The intersection. Counsel of the first, honesty of the last

The defensible asset is the record. Any competitor can seat a better model tomorrow; nobody can hand you two years of your own decisions with the confidence you claimed and the outcomes scored against it. That is switching cost, and unlike a model it compounds.

Three strengths are structural rather than asserted: genuine independence between advisers; calibration of the human rather than the model; and integrity defended at the system boundary.

Weaknesses, stated plainly:

  • Single-user. Without authentication yet.
  • The loop is slow by nature. The twentieth entry is where it begins telling you things, so retention through the cold period is the central product risk.
  • Outcomes are self-reported. The scoring has no referee but you.
  • Cost. The default gateway supports no prompt caching, putting cost per round at roughly 5× the design intent.
8Next steps
  • Correctness. Resolve one intermittent rendering defect in the synthesis view, and introduce schema migrations before the first change that must preserve real decisions.
  • Highest leverage. Per-domain calibration. You are well calibrated on Finance and badly overconfident on Career is the most valuable sentence this product could say, and the data to say it is already being recorded.
  • Adoption. Route questions to the right table automatically, and drive reviews from outside the app. The loop dies if reviews do not happen.
  • Scale. A shared event bus to permit more than one worker, authentication and multi-user, a cost model for the default provider, and an export path. The record is the user's most valuable asset and currently has no way out.
